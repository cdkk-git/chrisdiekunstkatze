Posted: October 21, 2026   |   Travel Log

Chres' Castle is now coming to you from Nashville, Tennessee. Nashville is the capital of the state and the county seat of Davidson County, sitting on the Cumberland River in Middle Tennessee. It was founded in 1779 by a party led by James Robertson and John Donelson, and it was named after Francis Nash, a Continental Army general from the American Revolutionary War. The river made it a busy port, and later a railroad center, and the state made it the permanent capital in 1843.

Most people know it as Music City, thanks to the country music industry and the Grand Ole Opry, which began in the 1920s. It also goes by the older nickname Athens of the South. Below is the flag of Nashville and Davidson County, in the style of this site.

Posted: October 21, 2026   |   Places

The Main Library is at 615 Church Street downtown. The building opened in 2001, was designed by Robert A.M. Stern Architects, and has a neoclassical front with Ionic columns and a central portico. Inside, the third floor has the Grand Reading Room, and the Special Collections division keeps archives and oral histories about Nashville's history. The Main Library puts on puppet shows by Wishing Chair Productions, and the system has 21 locations in total. In 2017 it was named Library of the Year.

One of its best-known programs is Limitless Libraries, which began as a 2009 pilot and delivers books and materials from the library to students in Metro Nashville Public Schools, right to their school library.

The library is a department of the city government, so extra programs depend on private giving. That is the job of the Nashville Public Library Foundation, a nonprofit founded in 1997 that raises private funds for library programs, buildings and collections. You can also join a Friends of the Library group, volunteer, or donate books and materials through the library's support page.

Posted: October 21, 2026   |   Poetry

The Academy of American Poets was founded in 1934 and is a 501(c)(3) charitable organization. It runs Poets.org, Poem-a-Day (a free daily publication of new poems), the American Poets Prizes and National Poetry Month each April, and it offers lesson plans and classroom resources for K-12 teachers through Teach This Poem. Its programs are free and are paid for by donations from readers.

A city on a river is a good reason to think about wild places too. The World Wildlife Fund (WWF) works in nearly 100 countries on wildlife, forests, oceans, fresh water, food and climate, and it is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. A donation to WWF counts as general support and makes you a member. You can also give by phone at 1-800-CALL-WWF.
